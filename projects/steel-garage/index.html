BIM & structural modelling

Steel garage in Revit

I modelled a 6 × 9 m steel garage in Revit, coordinating the pitched frame, bracing and bolted connections around a clear parking bay and side entrance.

My role
Revit modelling and detailing
Footprint
6 × 9 m
Layout
3 frames at 4.5 m centres
Roof pitch
18.4°

The completed Revit model with a terracotta steel roof, side entrance and driveway approach.

HOW I BUILT IT
01 / Set out the frame 02 / Set the roof pitch 03 / Resolve the joints 04 / Coordinate bracing and access 05 / See the finished model
01 / Grid and frame layout

Keep the parking space clear

I started with a 6 × 9 m grid. Three pitched frames spanned the 6 m width, with columns along the side walls and two 4.5 m bays along the length. That gave me a clear parking space and a repeated frame to detail.

What would a fourth frame change?

I compared the modelled grid with an evenly spaced four-frame option. A fourth frame shortens the distance between frames by 1.5 m, but adds two column and footing positions and another set of eaves and ridge joints.

Modelled layout

Three frames

2 bays · 6 columns · 6 pads

Concept comparison

Four frames

3 bays · 8 columns · 8 pads

I’d keep three frames for this concept. Both layouts leave the parking space clear. The three-frame grid leaves longer side-wall bays to work with when placing the door and bracing, with fewer joints to detail.

02 / Roof geometry

Give the rafters and roof one pitch

I set the eaves at 3 m and the ridge at 4 m. A 1 m rise over a 3 m half-span gives a pitch of 18.4° (arctan of 1 ÷ 3). I used that same pitch for the rafters and roof footprint so the two followed the same geometry.

03 / Steel connections

Work out why the joints didn’t fit

The eaves connection wouldn’t generate

I checked the rafter offsets first, then looked at the column in plan. Its web was rotated 90° relative to the rafter web. I rotated one column, checked that Revit generated the plates and bolts, then repeated the detail.

The rafters met with a step at the ridge

I inspected how Revit trimmed the rafter ends at the join. I then modelled a bolted apex connection so I could see how the rafters, end plates and bolts fit together.

04 / Bracing and access

Leave room for the side door

A wall diagonal in the front bay would interfere with the side entrance. I left that bay clear on the door side and used the adjacent bay for the wall diagonal. I hid the roof covering to inspect where the roof and wall braces met the frame.

Trace the intended wind load path

For wind acting along the garage, I traced an intended route through the roof diagonal, eaves junction and wall diagonal to the column base and footing. The points to check next are the connections at each transfer and how the bracing would behave with wind from the opposite direction.

05 / Finished model

From the first grid to the final model

I brought the exterior and roof framing together on one Revit sheet. Using the same viewpoint makes it easy to see where the bracing, steel frame and footings sit behind the finished garage.

Exterior and roof framing views from sheet C102, Garage Overview. Select either view to enlarge it.

What I took from the project

I learnt to check a decision from more than one view before carrying it through the model. The plan exposed the rotated column that stopped the eaves connection from generating. Hiding the roof let me check the brace locations against the side entrance.
