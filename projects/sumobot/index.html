The brief

The robot needed to house wheels and electronics inside a compact body, with a sloping top plate that could get underneath another robot. The exterior also needed openings for sensing, assembly and access.

My contribution

In 2025, I designed the mechanical model in Autodesk AutoCAD and helped fabricate and assemble the robot in the UNSW Makerspace. Teammates were responsible for Arduino programming, and we worked together to integrate the mechanical and electronic systems.

Decisions that shaped the work

01

Choose a wedge for the lifting strategy

A flat front would rely mainly on pushing. A separate lifting mechanism would add moving parts and use space needed for wheels and electronics. We chose a fixed wedge so forward motion could help the sloping plate get underneath and lift an opponent, with the aim of overturning it.

02

Bring the leading edge close to the ground

The sloping plate needed a low leading edge to reach underneath another robot. The front tip extends past the red casing so the body does not obstruct that approach.

03

Allow clearance around moving parts

The CAD model uses simplified wheel and motor solids to check packaging. The bottom-cover wheel openings are 26 × 34 mm, providing a buffer around the estimated wheel dimensions.

04

Make the underside accessible

The bottom-cover design uses four mounting pads, M3 screw clearance holes, hexagonal nut pockets and recessed screw heads to allow access to the interior.

Three ways to engage an opponent

We wanted the robot to get underneath an opponent and lift its front edge. The two bumper concepts illustrate different ways to make contact. Both use the same 100 mm-wide chassis and 1 mm nominal bumper clearance.

Flat-front pusher

A full-width vertical face pushes an opponent straight ahead.

Angled bumper

A 35 mm setback across the 100 mm chassis angles the bumper by 19.3°. One corner makes first contact to try to turn the opponent sideways.

Fixed wedge

We chose the sloping plate so the robot could get under an opponent and lift its front edge as it drove forward.
