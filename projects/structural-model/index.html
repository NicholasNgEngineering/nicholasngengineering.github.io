The brief

Our five-person team designed and built a lightweight building model for the DESN1000 Shock-Resistant Buildings challenge. The brief called for a structure taller than 500 mm, within a 300 × 300 mm site and below 400 g. It had to support a 5 kg dead load, face a toy-vehicle impact and consider the loss of 25% of its ground-level columns, while protecting an egg suspended inside.

My contribution

I worked with a five-person team to evaluate three concepts against seven criteria and develop the selected design. We combined an X-braced trapezoidal frame with a truss base from an alternative concept. My work included SPACE GASS modelling of static loading and the removal of one of four main columns, alongside hands-on fabrication and assembly with teammates.

Decisions that shaped the work

01

Make the concept selection explicit

We compared a circular central-column concept, an X-braced trapezoidal frame and a rectangular frame with a truss base. A pros-and-cons review, pairwise comparison and weighted decision matrix helped balance strength, impact resistance, progressive-collapse resistance, material efficiency, aesthetics, constructability and sustainability.

02

Combine the frame with a stronger base strategy

The trapezoidal concept ranked highest in our decision matrix. We then adopted the truss base from the rectangular concept to improve the intended load path after a lower support was lost. The X-bracing provided diagonal paths between adjacent members.

03

Build the connections as carefully as the members

We cut and fitted balsa members into paired frames, temporarily taped the assembly for alignment, and used epoxy and clamps to secure the joints. Dado joints allowed crossing braces to sit flush. A paper basket suspended with dental floss protected the egg inside.

04

Track the weight of the whole assembly

The structural model estimated approximately 350 g, while the finished prototype weighed 388 g. The report attributes much of the difference to the facade, estimated at 30–40 g, with further variation from glue, cutting and sanding. The final build remained 12 g below the 400 g limit.

We completed a 388 g prototype and documented its construction, SPACE GASS analysis and physical impact-test setup. The report describes stable behaviour in the static model and alternative load paths after a column was removed. Physical testing was discussed qualitatively, without a tabulated impact capacity or measured deflection result. This was a student-scale exploration of structural robustness; further dynamic testing and connection assessment were identified as next steps.

Connecting the model to the build

Static loading

The SPACE GASS analysis applied a total downward load of 50 N, split as 12.5 N across each of four top nodes. This approximated the 5 kg dead-load target. Ground supports restrained translation while allowing rotation.

Loss of a lower support

The team examined the removal of one of four main columns. The analysis explored how the bottom truss could transfer load across the base, with internal and external X-bracing providing diagonal paths to adjacent members.

What the prototype added

Fabrication made the weight of the facade, the fit of the braces and the quality of glued connections tangible. The report also identifies connection strength as a limitation, and recommends more complex loading, shake-table and cyclic tests for further investigation.
